# Hearth — Aesthetic Profile

**Phase 1 · The brief of briefs · Authored by Nikki T.**

This document outranks every other design document in the project. If `01-references.md`, `02-design-brief.md`, or `03-information-architecture.md` conflict with this profile, *they* are wrong. This is the immutable taste contract for Hearth.

---

The overall feeling is **quiet competence**. Not a wellness app trying to look like a spa, not a clinical tool trying to look like a hospital. Closer to a well-designed reading app or a thoughtful journal — something that respects the user's intelligence and doesn't perform care at them.

## Mood

Calm but not sleepy. Warm but not soft-focus. The app should feel like *a room with good light and one chair that's clearly meant for you.* Restrained, never empty. Honest about what it is.

## Palette

Mostly muted, low-saturation. Think the colors you'd find in **natural materials seen at dusk** — paper, unbleached linen, river stone, slate, faded ink, dried sage. One quiet accent that does almost all the chromatic work; everything else lives in a narrow tonal range.

Dark mode isn't an inversion — it's its own composition — closer to **deep indigo or warm charcoal** than pure black.

**Avoid:** lavender-and-mint wellness gradients, hospital teal, "calm" blue that's actually corporate SaaS blue.

## Typography

A **serious serif** for anything the user reads (entries, prompts, reflections) — something with **warmth and a quiet personality**, not a generic system serif. A **clean humanist sans** for UI. Generous line height. Type is allowed to be large where it matters. Numbers and timestamps tucked into the background, never shouting.

## Layout

Lots of negative space, but the kind that feels **intentional rather than under-designed**. One thing on screen at a time when the moment calls for it. No dashboards. No streaks displayed like a Duolingo trophy case. **Information hierarchy follows emotional hierarchy.**

## Motion

Slow, eased, **never bouncy**. Transitions feel like **turning a page**, not swiping a tile. Nothing celebratory — no confetti, no haptic high-fives for logging a hard day.

## Voice

Plainspoken. Adult. Doesn't call the user "friend." Doesn't ask "how are we feeling today?" **Doesn't use the word *journey*.** When it asks something, it asks like a thoughtful person would, not like an intake form.

## Materials and references

**Closer to:** Kinfolk magazine, Aesop, Freitag, the Calm-before-it-got-loud era, old Moleskine packaging, the Eames House interior, libraries.

**Further from:** Headspace mascots, gradient meditation apps, anything that looks like a Notion template.

## The line it walks

Premium without being precious. Sincere without being sentimental. Designed enough that the care is visible, undesigned enough that nothing feels marketed. The user should sense that someone made this for **grown-ups who are taking themselves seriously.**

---

## Operating rules derived from this profile

These are the binary tests that any design decision must pass.

1. **The accent test.** Could one of the muted natural materials in the palette description (paper, unbleached linen, river stone, slate, faded ink, dried sage) sit next to this color without clashing? If no, the color is wrong.
2. **The motion test.** Does this animation feel like turning a page in a book, or like a UI element snapping into place? If it's the second one, slow it down or remove it.
3. **The voice test.** Could a thoughtful adult say this sentence to another thoughtful adult, in a quiet room, without sounding ridiculous? If no, rewrite.
4. **The reference test.** Could this screen appear in Kinfolk magazine or an Aesop store as visual material without anyone noticing it was an app? If no, push further toward natural-material restraint.
5. **The anti-reference test.** Could a Headspace user mistake this for Headspace? If yes, immediately rewrite.
6. **The chair test.** When the user opens this screen, does it feel like the room was prepared for them? Or does it feel like they walked into a busy office?
