## 1. Executive Summary

Hearth is the first desktop-native psychological operating system — an application that builds a clinical-grade psychological profile and actively reconfigures the computing environment based on real-time psychological state. Unlike mobile wellness apps that track symptoms and offer suggestions, Hearth exists at the OS layer: it kills applications when anxiety spikes, dims the display when depression flattens energy, enforces Do Not Disturb during manic windows, and reorganizes the file system to match cognitive capacity. The product serves people managing ADHD, anxiety, depression, OCD, PTSD, and bipolar disorder who have abandoned browser-based trackers because tracking does not change outcomes. Hearth operates on a freemium model: core mental health tools remain free forever, while autonomous execution, custom automation rules, and longitudinal analytics drive paid tiers at $8/month (Pro) and $15/month (Premium).

## 2. Brand Purpose & Vision

### Purpose
We exist because the computing environment is the most influential and least accountable force in modern mental health. Every notification, open tab, and bright pixel is a stimulus with clinical consequences — and until now, no tool has treated the desktop as a therapeutic intervention surface. Hearth makes the computer itself a partner in psychological stability.

### Vision
A world where people with psychiatric conditions do not spend cognitive energy fighting their tools. Where the devices they work on recognize their state and modify themselves accordingly — quietly, without spectacle, with the same inevitability as a thermostat responding to temperature.

---

## 3. Mission Statement

To embed evidence-based mental health support into the operating system layer, making proactive environmental adaptation the default behavior of personal computing — not a feature to be remembered, launched, and maintained.

## 4. Brand Values

### 1. Action Over Awareness
We do not believe in insight without intervention. A mood score that sits in a database is a failed intervention. Every data point in Hearth must trigger a mechanical or environmental response: close the application, change the theme, mute the channel, dim the screen. Awareness is the starting line. Action is the product.

**Behavioral Manifestation:**
- Free tier users receive suggestions that are immediately executable in one click.
- Every tracked metric has a corresponding automation rule.
- Analytics are not presented as charts for contemplation; they are presented as calibration inputs for behavior change.

### 2. System-Level Integrity
We do not add features at the UI layer and pretend they change the environment. If Hearth cannot modify the actual computing context — brightness, process list, notification state, file system — the feature does not ship.

**Behavioral Manifestation:**
- The product requires desktop-native architecture; web or mobile versions that cannot access system APIs are not equivalent offerings.
- Automation rules are tested against real system states, not mocked UI states.
- Documentation is explicit about what the system can and cannot control.

### 3. Evidence-Based Ruthlessness
Our automation rules are derived from clinical literature, not from productivity folklore or Silicon Valley intuition. If a rule cannot be traced to a peer-reviewed mechanism, it is experimental and labeled as such.

**Behavioral Manifestation:**
- Each of the 14 core automation rules cites its clinical origin in the knowledge base.
- Features derived from CBT, DBT, ACT, and ERP are implemented by condition, not by generalization.
- The product does not invent therapeutic frameworks; it operationalizes existing ones.

### 4. Dignity Through Invisibility
Mental health support should not require performance. Hearth never congratulates the user for self-care, never displays streaks in shared spaces, and never forces an interaction when the user is in crisis. The most effective intervention is often the one the user does not notice.

**Behavioral Manifestation:**
- Crisis states trigger environmental changes without dialog boxes or confirmation prompts.
- Gamification exists only in the Pro tier and can be fully disabled.
- Notifications are opt-in and context-aware; the product never pings for engagement.
- Default themes avoid the visual language of medical or wellness apps.

### 5. Core Access as Infrastructure
Basic mental health tooling is not a luxury good. The tracking, logging, therapeutic exercises, and crisis planning that keep people stable belong in the public infrastructure of the product, not behind a paywall.

**Behavioral Manifestation:**
- Free tier includes unlimited mood logging, DBT diary cards, medication tracking, breathing and grounding exercises, crisis plans, and guided meditations.
- Revenue is derived from execution infrastructure (autonomous rules, custom logic, cross-device sync) and convenience (analytics export, reporting), not from access to therapeutic tools.
- Pricing pages never frame free features as "basic" or "limited" — they are framed as "core."

## 5. Brand Personality & Voice

### Personality
Hearth behaves like a competent bodyguard who has worked with you long enough to anticipate trouble. Not chatty. Not clinical. Not optimistic. Observant, decisive, and slightly ahead of the moment.

- **Quiet, not silent.** Speaks when there is something to say; otherwise modifies the environment without announcement.
- **Firm, not authoritarian.** Makes protective decisions on the user's behalf but always leaves an undo path.
- **Attentive, not surveillance-oriented.** Collects data only to act on it, never to profile for advertising or engagement.
- **Mechanically empathetic.** Understands suffering through behavioral signals, not through sentiment analysis or chatbot conversation.

### Voice

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| In-app instructions | Direct, minimal | "Discord will close in 30 seconds. Press Cancel to keep it open." |
| Crisis intervention | Calm, operational | "Your screen is dimming. Your crisis plan is one click away." |
| Error states | Non-judgmental, specific | "Rule failed: Telegram is not running. No action taken." |
| Marketing copy | Specific, grounded | "Your computer should adapt to your psychology, not the other way around." |
| Onboarding | Plain, respectful | "Which conditions are you managing? This changes what Hearth watches for." |

### Voice Rules
- **Never infantilize.** No "Hey there!" No exclamation points in system messages. No emoji in default UI copy.
- **Never medicalize.** We do not diagnose, prognose, or use clinical language to imply authority. We use clinical research to inform mechanical actions.
- **Never optimize.** The word and concept are excluded. We do not make users "better." We make their environment match their state.
- **Never celebrate for show.** No confetti for streaks. No praise for logging. The reward is a system that works better tomorrow.

## 6. Positioning Statement

For desktop workers managing ADHD, anxiety, depression, OCD, PTSD, or bipolar disorder, Hearth is the desktop-native psychological operating system that reconfigures the computing environment in real time based on psychological state. Unlike mobile mood trackers or browser-based CBT apps that require the user to remember to engage, Hearth acts proactively at the OS layer — closing applications, adjusting brightness, enforcing focus states, and reorganizing files before the user recognizes they need it. Built on 14 evidence-based automation rules and an ethical freemium model that keeps core therapeutic tools free forever, Hearth treats the computer itself as the intervention surface.

### Key Differentiation Summary
No competitor combines **tracking + profiling + proactive system adaptation**. The market splits cleanly into:
- **Trackers** (Bearable, Daylio, eMoods) — collect data, display it back
- **Intervention libraries** (Sanvello, Headspace) — provide content the user must choose to engage
- **Single-purpose utilities** (Freedom, f.lux) — modify one system parameter reactively

Hearth is the only product that builds a profile, monitors state, and modifies the entire computing environment autonomously. This is not a feature difference; it is a category difference.

## 8. Target Audience Definition

### Primary Audience: The Self-Managing Desktop Worker

**Demographics**
- Age 22–45
- Uses a desktop or laptop computer for 4+ hours daily
- Manages one or more of: ADHD, anxiety, depression, OCD, PTSD, bipolar disorder
- Has tried 2+ mental health apps and abandoned them due to "tracking fatigue"

**Psychographics**
- Does not want to "work on their mental health" as a separate job
- Experiences their computer as a source of both livelihood and psychological friction
- Has sufficient self-awareness to recognize patterns (e.g., "I doomscroll when anxious") but insufficient environmental control to stop them
- Skeptical of "wellness" branding and productivity culture
- Willing to grant system access to an application that demonstrates concrete, immediate behavioral outcomes

**Behavioral Signals**
- Has installed and uninstalled Freedom, Cold Turkey, or similar focus apps
- Uses f.lux or Night Shift but wishes it responded to mood, not just time
- Maintains a notes file or spreadsheet for mood tracking because dedicated apps felt like too much work
- Has strong opinions about notification settings but changes them manually and inconsistently

### Secondary Audience: The Support Network

**Demographics**
- Partners, parents, or close friends of primary audience members
- Age 30–60
- Concerned about loved one's digital habits but does not want to surveil

**Psychographics**
- Wants to support without becoming the "tech police"
- Values tools that reduce their own emotional labor (e.g., not having to remind partner to take breaks)
- Premium tier's support network sharing and analytics reports appeal to this group

### Audience Exclusions
Hearth does not target:
- **Corporate wellness buyers** seeking employee productivity dashboards
- **People seeking diagnosis** or clinical treatment (the product is not a medical device)
- **Mobile-first users** who do not use a desktop computer for extended work sessions
- **Optimization enthusiasts** pursuing peak performance (the product serves stability, not peaks)

## 9. Brand Promise

**To every user:** Your computer will meet you where you are — not where an algorithm thinks you should be.

This promise has three enforceable commitments:

1. **Environmental Fidelity:** If Hearth knows your state, it will change your environment to match it. No suggestions without execution paths. No data without consequences.

2. **Dignified Defaults:** You will never be asked to perform wellness for an audience. Core tools are free. Crisis support is instant. Gamification is optional. Your data stays on your device unless you explicitly choose otherwise.

3. **Mechanical Empathy:** Hearth does not care if you are productive. It cares if your environment is compatible with your current psychological reality. When that reality shifts — from focus to panic, from energy to exhaustion — your computer will shift with it, quietly, without asking you to manage the transition yourself.

---

*Hearth is not a wellness app. It is not a therapist. It is an operating system that finally acknowledges the person sitting in front of it.*
