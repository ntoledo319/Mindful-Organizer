## 2. The separate, non-legal problem: the name is bad for discovery

Independent of any trademark question, "Hearth" is a weak Store search term:

- "Hearth" queries in app and gaming contexts are saturated by **Hearthstone**
  and its ecosystem of trackers and companions. A user searching "hearth" is
  overwhelmingly not looking for a planner.
- The productivity meaning of the word is already occupied by Hearth Display.

For a product whose entire distribution plan is organic Store discovery plus a
handful of owner-authored posts — no ad budget — the name is competing for a
term where it will lose to a Blizzard franchise and a funded startup. That is a
marketing cost being paid every day the listing is live, and it is unrelated to
whether anyone ever sends a letter.

## 3. What a rename actually costs

Measured, not estimated. 117 files and 606 occurrences of `Hearth`/`hearth`
outside `node_modules`, build output, and caches.

**The binding constraint is package identity.** `store/identity.json` pins
`identityName: ToledoTechnologies.Hearth` against reserved Partner Center
product `9PLRSZZMFPJH`. Any user-visible rename changes `productName`, which
changes the AppX manifest, which changes the package bytes. Consequences:

- **CAND-002 dies.** New candidate cycle, new AppX, new SHA-256, new
  screenshots. The staged `tmp/CAND-002-SWAP/` kit becomes waste.
- A name must be reserved in Partner Center (owner action, minutes).
- The rest is mechanical: `package.json` productName, `store/identity.json`,
  `store/listing-metadata.json`, UI strings, docs, landing copy, issue
  templates. Machine work, one pass, all gates re-run.

**Cost now:** one CI candidate cycle (~16 min machine time), one owner name
reservation, one agent sweep. Nothing is live, so nothing breaks.

**Cost after publication:** the Store URL, the listing's review history, the
installed base, every campaign link in `store/CAMPAIGNS.md` and
`store/LAUNCH_TARGETS.md`, and any coverage earned by the launch batch. Plus
whatever the collision itself costs.

The asymmetry is the whole point. Right now a rename is cheap and reversible.
Three days after a purchasable listing exists, it is neither.

## 4. What Microsoft itself says — the actual mechanism

Added 2026-08-07 after checking Microsoft Learn's app-naming guidance rather
than speculating about it. Microsoft states plainly that developers must not use
names trademarked by others, that a rights holder **can report an infringement**,
and that if this happens **after publication the app is removed from the Store**
until every instance of the name is changed in the app, its content, and its
listing — and then it must be **submitted for certification again**.

That is the concrete downside, and it is not a lawsuit. It is:

1. listing pulled,
2. full rename sweep under time pressure,
3. new package, new hash, new candidate cycle,
4. re-certification,
5. customers who bought a product that vanished,
6. every campaign link dead.

Doing that voluntarily today costs one CI cycle. Doing it under a takedown, with
a purchasable page and buyers attached, costs the launch. **The asymmetry is
Microsoft's own documented process, not a hypothetical.**

## 5. Can you just call it Hearth anyway?

Probably, on the law. Set out honestly, because the earlier framing in this
repository overstated the risk:

**Arguments that you can:**

- Trademark rights are not ownership of a word. They protect against likelihood
  of confusion among consumers in related markets.
- The filed mark is **HEARTH DISPLAY** — two words, for a wall-mounted family
  touchscreen with a companion app and subscription. Yours is a single-user
  Windows desktop app, local-only, one-time purchase, no sync. Different device,
  buyer, channel, and use case.
- "Hearth" is a common English word used suggestively (home, warmth). Such marks
  get narrower protection than coined terms.
- The name is already reserved in Partner Center as product `9PLRSZZMFPJH`. Not
  clearance, but nobody else has claimed it in that Store.

**Arguments for caution:**

- Both products sit under personal/household organization software, and the
  IC 009 description overlaps this product closely.
- Hearth Display markets under **"Hearth" standalone** and holds a Google Play
  developer account under that short form — they assert more than the two-word
  mark.
- They are funded, which means counsel and an incentive to police.
- Status of 97524800 is unknown. If it registered in IC 009, the picture tightens.

**Bottom line:** the legal case for renaming is real but weak-to-moderate, and
unresolvable without the tmsearch status check. **The stronger argument was
never legal — it is discovery.** See `store/DISCOVERY-REVIEW-2026-08-07.md`:
"Hearth" competes for a query term owned by Hearthstone, with the productivity
meaning owned by Hearth Display, for a product with no ad budget whose entire
plan is organic Store search. That cost is paid daily and does not depend on
anyone's legal opinion.
